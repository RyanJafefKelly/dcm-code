# Deep Research Prompt: Bayesian Workflow for Synthetic Data Validation

I want a deep research report on best practices for synthetic-data validation,
parameter recovery, and sensitivity analysis in Bayesian hierarchical models.

Context: I am working on a Bayesian hierarchical model with latent variables,
tree-structured dependencies, ordinal observation models, expert/rater effects,
and limited real data. I need to design synthetic-data checks that tell me
which quantities are recoverable, which are prior-driven, and which modelling
choices are merely interpretational rather than empirically identifiable.

Please treat this as a methodological research task, not a request for generic
advice. Use the Bayesian workflow literature and adjacent literatures on
simulation-based calibration, prior/posterior predictive checking, parameter
recovery, practical identifiability, Bayesian experimental design, and model
misspecification.

## Research Questions

1. What are the best-practice principles for synthetic-data validation in
   Bayesian hierarchical models?
2. How should one distinguish:
   - computational correctness;
   - calibration under the assumed model;
   - practical identifiability;
   - robustness to misspecification;
   - usefulness for scientific interpretation?
3. When is simulation-based calibration the right tool, and when is it
   overkill or misleading?
4. How should one design simulation studies when the model is expensive to fit
   and the full design grid is infeasible?
5. What metrics should be used for:
   - continuous parameters;
   - probabilities;
   - latent binary states;
   - derived quantities;
   - decision-relevant estimands;
   - posterior predictive performance?
6. How should prior sensitivity and prior-posterior conflict be integrated into
   synthetic validation?
7. How should one evaluate whether a latent structure is scientifically
   meaningful versus merely a weakly identified modelling convenience?
8. How should synthetic validation inform data-collection decisions, including
   sample size, rater overlap, reference systems, and expected information gain?

## Deliverables

Please produce a structured report with:

1. An executive summary of the core principles.
2. A taxonomy of synthetic validation tasks, distinguishing:
   - prior predictive checks;
   - fake-data parameter recovery;
   - simulation-based calibration;
   - posterior predictive checks;
   - model-misspecification simulations;
   - sensitivity analysis;
   - Bayesian experimental design.
3. A recommended workflow for a two-day validation sprint.
4. A recommended workflow for a larger, publication-quality validation study.
5. A table of estimands and diagnostics, including:
   - bias;
   - RMSE / MAE;
   - credible interval coverage;
   - interval width;
   - posterior contraction;
   - posterior entropy reduction;
   - rank calibration / SBC ranks;
   - Brier score;
   - log score;
   - expected information gain;
   - posterior predictive discrepancies.
6. Guidance on which metrics are primary, secondary, or easy to misuse.
7. Specific guidance for latent binary variables:
   - how to score posterior probabilities of latent states;
   - how to avoid overinterpreting classification accuracy;
   - how to measure practical identifiability;
   - how to decide whether binary latent variables are scientifically
     meaningful.
8. Specific guidance for derived quantities:
   - when derived estimands should be prioritised over primitive parameters;
   - how to validate nonlinear or path-product quantities;
   - how to handle sign recovery and threshold decisions.
9. Guidance on model-mismatch simulation:
   - fitting model A to data generated by model B;
   - testing discrete latent models on continuous-latent synthetic truths;
   - testing robustness to rater heterogeneity and observation-layer
     misspecification.
10. A practical design template:
    - minimal number of simulation seeds;
    - how to choose parameter settings;
    - how to choose sample-size/design scenarios;
    - when to use full model fits versus reduced toy models;
    - how to triage expensive fits.
11. A section on Bayesian experimental design:
    - expected information gain;
    - expected posterior interval width;
    - utility functions for decision-relevant estimands;
    - when approximate design calculations are good enough.
12. A concise checklist I can use before starting implementation.

## Literature Requirements

Please cite and briefly explain the relevance of key sources. Include, where
relevant:

- Gelman et al. on Bayesian workflow and posterior predictive checking.
- Talts et al. on simulation-based calibration.
- Betancourt on principled Bayesian workflow, prior predictive checks, and
  identifiability.
- Literature on Bayesian experimental design / expected information gain.
- Literature on latent variable model identifiability and parameter recovery.
- Any relevant modern applied Bayesian guidance for hierarchical models,
  ordinal models, or psychometric / item-response-style settings.

Prioritise sources that are methodologically rigorous and practically useful.
If there are disagreements or caveats in the literature, explain them.

## Style

Write for a statistically literate Bayesian modeller. Skip introductory Bayes
exposition. Be concrete, critical, and implementation-oriented. Emphasise what
will actually change modelling or data-collection decisions.
